# Design System: Alexandre Akira Portfolio

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Repertoire"**

One performer, seven staged productions of the same script. The content (name, title, tagline, experience, skills, projects, blog) never changes; a switch in the header restages it in one of seven complete visual worlds: Avant-garde (the default), Engineer, Creative, Editorial, Arcade, Night Shift, and Metro. The system's personality is plural on purpose. Each world commits fully to its own register (the terminal, the studio, the dark gallery, the broadsheet, the arcade cabinet, the warehouse floor at 3 a.m., the metro line). None of them is a theme laid over a neutral base, because the neutral base does not exist.

Underneath, one skeleton carries all seven: a fixed translucent header, a full-viewport hero, and a single centered column of sections (About, Experience, Education, Skills, Projects, Contact) at medium density. A world declares nine semantic color tokens (page, raised, text, secondary text, accent, accent hover, border, card, card hover), two font slots, and two radii, and the shared sections re-skin themselves. Only Hero, Experience, and Skills get a bespoke component per world; Metro alone adds a page-level overlay, the fixed line that indexes the sections.

The aesthetic philosophy is restraint everywhere except the accent and the display type. Every world has exactly one accent and spends it on small, high-signal things: buttons, period labels, rules, hover and selection states. Display typography carries identity. Motion is one shared reveal grammar (fade and rise on scroll, once) plus a single signature per world. Six signatures live in the hero; Metro's runs the length of the page, one scroll value driving the train marker, the station doors, and the rail.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Seven complete worlds, one content source, switched at runtime and persisted per visitor.
- One accent per world, spent on interactive and labeling elements only.
- Display typography carries identity: monospace, geometric sans, heavy grotesk, display grotesk, slab poster, condensed signage, or black transit grotesk.
- Flat, border-defined surfaces in six worlds; Creative alone lifts with shadow.
- Shared reveal motion (fade, 30px rise, 0.5s, once) with a signature per world: six in the hero, Metro's along the whole scroll.
- WebGL in two worlds (Avant-garde's particle field, Night Shift's warehouse), each mounted only while its world is active and backed by a CSS fallback.
- Scene materials stay in the scene: Arcade's candy and Night Shift's sodium, safety yellow, and steel never become UI chrome.
- Bilingual by construction: every text node renders both locales and CSS shows one.

### Named Rules
**The One Accent Rule.** Each world has exactly one accent and spends it on buttons, labels, rules, hover and selection states. Body copy, headings, and card surfaces never take the accent. Arcade's candy colors are surfaces with Cabinet Black text on them, never accents, and Night Shift's scene materials never leave the canvas.

**The Own Palette Rule.** A world uses only its own token set. No Candy Green in Engineer, no Creative shadow in Editorial, no Terminal Teal outside the terminal.

**The Dark Text on Bright Accent Rule.** Teal, Lime, and Gold are light accents, so filled buttons put the page color on them (11:1 to 16:1). Violet takes white (6.7:1). Signal Orange is the exception that needs care: it measures 3.0:1 on Newsprint and 3.3:1 under white text, so it lives on bold headings, rules, outlines, and uppercase button labels. Blue Spot takes Asphalt (5.4:1). Linha Verde is the one accent that takes white (4.6:1) and reads 4.3:1 on Enamel, so green text stays bold or uppercase.

**The Three Lights Rule.** Night Shift's warehouse is lit by exactly three light families: Sodium overheads, one roaming Blue Spot, and the packets' own glow. Ambient stays at floor level (0.14) and nothing else emits.

## Typography

**Display Font:** per world. Engineer: JetBrains Mono (monospace). Creative: Sora (sans-serif). Avant-garde: Poppins (sans-serif). Editorial: Clash Display (sans-serif, served from Fontshare). Arcade: Bungee (sans-serif, single weight). Night Shift: Barlow Condensed (sans-serif, 700 and 800 loaded). Metro: Overpass Variable (sans-serif).
**Body Font:** per world. Engineer: Inter (sans-serif). Creative: Source Serif 4 (serif). Avant-garde: Inter (sans-serif). Editorial: DM Sans Variable (sans-serif). Arcade: Outfit (sans-serif). Night Shift: Barlow (sans-serif, 400 to 600). Metro: Overpass Variable (sans-serif), the first world with one family in both slots.
**Label/Mono Font:** JetBrains Mono (monospace) for the design switcher label, inline code, code blocks, all of Engineer's labels, and Night Shift's HUD readout and bay counts. Metro's timetable, period, and station labels use Overpass Mono (monospace) instead.

**Character:** The display slot carries each world's identity. Monospace reads as a prompt, Sora as a rounded modern studio, Poppins as a heavy geometric gallery wall, Clash Display as a broadsheet masthead, Bungee as a marquee sign, Barlow Condensed in caps as warehouse signage, Overpass at 900 as a platform sign. Body faces stay quiet and legible so the display can be loud. Every heading in a shared section resolves through the heading slot, so re-skinning changes the voice without touching a component.

- **Hero role line**: 1.25rem to 2.25rem depending on world, in secondary text. Engineer prefixes "> ". Arcade sets it in Bungee uppercase Coin Gold. Editorial pairs it with a 3px orange rule. Night Shift sets it in Barlow 500 uppercase at 1.25rem with 0.08em tracking. ### Named Rules
**The Heading Slot Rule.** Headings never hardcode a family. They use the heading slot (`--font-heading`) so a world can swap the voice in one token.

**The Loud Display, Quiet Body Rule.** Identity lives in the display size and face. Body and label sizes stay between 0.75rem and 1.125rem in every world.

**The Sign Rule.** In Metro every heading is a sign: h1 through h3 sit at weight 900, and section titles are set white on Linha Verde. Green text elsewhere is bold or uppercase.

## Elevation & Depth

There is no system-wide elevation model; each world decides. Six of the seven convey depth with 1px borders and one step of surface tone (page, raised, card, card hover). Creative is the only world that lifts.

- **Engineer:** flat. Borders in Navy Line and one raised tone. The only depth cue is the 40px grid drawn on the page.
- **Creative:** lifted. Cards carry a soft ambient shadow, the portrait a deep one, chips a hairline shadow that grows on hover. Cards and chips also rise 4px on hover.
- **Avant-garde:** flat with glow. Borders in Charcoal Line, a 3% noise overlay for material, and a 16px Acid Lime glow at 12% on chip hover.
- **Editorial:** flat, hairline only. Depth comes from ghost numerals at 4% to 6% and hairlines that draw in on reveal.
- **Arcade:** flat and saturated. Candy surfaces sit directly on Cabinet Black with no border or shadow.
- **Night Shift:** flat, lit by its scene. 1px Seam borders, one tone step, and no shadow anywhere in the UI; the only light in the world is the WebGL floor behind the hero. A hovered inventory bay takes a 1px inset Blue Spot ring.
- **Metro:** flat, hairline only. 1px Tile Line borders and white cards on Enamel. The platform doors carry a 4px Linha Verde edge painted with an inset box-shadow, which is a stripe, not depth.

Floating layers (the design dropdown, the mobile panel, blog card hover) use one soft shadow in every world.

## Do's and Don'ts
