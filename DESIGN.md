# Design System: Alexandre Akira Portfolio

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Repertoire"**

One performer, eight staged productions of the same script. The content (name, title, tagline, experience, skills, projects, blog) never changes; a switch in the header restages it in one of eight complete visual worlds: Editorial (the default), Avant-garde, Engineer, Arcade, Terminal, Tape, Cartoon, and Diorama. The system's personality is plural on purpose. Each world commits fully to its own register (the broadsheet, the dark gallery, the Internet-Draft read in a pager, the arcade cabinet, the shell session, the punched teletype tape, the opening titles of a 1960s cartoon, and one working day in a miniature room seen from outside). None of them is a theme laid over a neutral base, because the neutral base does not exist.

Underneath, one skeleton carries all eight: a fixed translucent header, a full-viewport hero, and a single centered column of sections (About, Experience, Education, Skills, Projects, Contact) at medium density. A world declares nine semantic color tokens (page, raised, text, secondary text, accent, accent hover, border, card, card hover), two font slots, and two radii, and the shared sections re-skin themselves. Two accent-use tokens sit on top for every world: `--on-accent`, the text on an accent fill (the page color by default), and `--accent-text`, small text in the accent (the accent itself by default); a world overrides either only when its accent needs it, as Editorial, Engineer, and Diorama do. A world may add tokens of its own beside the nine when its material needs them (Terminal's prompt green and bright text, Tape's punched windows, Cartoon's five paper fields, Diorama's paper, its ink, and the hour's ground); they never leave that world. Every world stages its own Hero, Experience, and Skills; Editorial, Engineer, Terminal, Tape, Cartoon, and Diorama also stage their own About and footer, and the last five their own Education, Projects, and Contact.

The aesthetic philosophy is restraint everywhere except the accent and the display type. Every world spends its accent on small, high-signal things: buttons, period labels, rules, hover and selection states. Cartoon turns the strategy over: colour is the field, not the accent; every section is a title card drenched in one of five flat paper colours, and on each card the accent is the one colour that card names safe for small text. Display typography carries identity, except in Engineer, where one face at one size carries it and hierarchy comes from numbering, position, indent, and bold. Motion is one shared reveal grammar (fade and rise on scroll, once) plus a single signature per world, staged in the hero and in its curtain; Cartoon replaces the tween with poses held on twos. Diorama replaces the reveal with a camera: one fixed 3D stage holds Akira's room for the whole page, every section is an hour of his working day, and scroll is the clock; on wide screens the room stands in a fixed panel and the day is a paper page beside it, read like a planner with every hour in its margin, and the accent is the lamp.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Eight complete worlds, one content source, switched at runtime and persisted per visitor.
- One accent per world, spent on interactive and labeling elements only; Terminal's prompt green is its one sanctioned second voice.
- Display typography carries identity: monospace, typewriter, heavy grotesk, display grotesk, slab poster, condensed caps, hand-lettered cartoon titling, or a tight heavy grotesk beside thin mono clock numerals.
- Flat surfaces in every world: border-defined in six, drenched cut-paper fields in Cartoon, where depth is only paper lying on paper, a flat paper page beside a lit 3D room in Diorama, where the only cast shadows are the room's own. No world lifts its surfaces with shadow.
- Shared reveal motion (fade, 30px rise, 0.5s, once) with a signature per world, staged in the hero.
- WebGL in two worlds (Avant-garde's particle field, Diorama's room), each mounted only while its world is active and backed by a fallback (CSS dots; a still of the room at 08:00).
- Candy stays on the cards: Arcade's candy colors are surfaces, never UI chrome. Cartoon's fields are surfaces too, one per section.
- Limited animation in Cartoon: poses held on twos (`steps()`), never tweened.
- Scroll is the clock in Diorama: one fixed stage, one station per section, the hour written by the stage into a clock in the room's top-right corner, and printed in the page's margin beside each hour.
- Bilingual by construction: every text node renders both locales and CSS shows one.

## Colors

Eight self-contained palettes, each a dark or paper neutral family plus one accent; Arcade adds a set of candy surfaces, Terminal a prompt green, Tape a family of punched white windows, Cartoon five flat paper fields that its cards are cut from, Diorama a paper page in ink beside a dark ground that takes the hour's sky.

### Primary
- **Link Blue** (#1d4ed8): Engineer's accent. Every link (underlined at a 0.18em offset), the `[+] details` toggles, a dot-leader line's title on hover, the "<A4 />" mark, and the one filled control, the Email key, with white text (`--on-accent`, 6.70:1). ### Named Rules
**The One Accent Rule.** Each world has exactly one accent and spends it on buttons, labels, rules, hover and selection states. Body copy, headings, and card surfaces never take the accent. Arcade's candy colors are surfaces with Cabinet Black text on them, never accents. Terminal's prompt green is the shell's own prompt colour, held to the prompt user, active periods, JSON values, and the email key; it never becomes a second link or hover colour. Cartoon's fields are surfaces, like the candy; on each card the accent is the one safe colour that card names. Diorama's Lamp Ink is Lamp Amber carried onto paper, never a second accent on the ground.

**The Own Palette Rule.** A world uses only its own token set, world-only tokens included. No Candy Green in Engineer, no Tape Orange in Editorial or Signal Orange in Tape, no prompt green outside the terminal, no Cartoon field outside Cartoon, no Diorama paper card or lamp outside Diorama.

**The Dark Text on Bright Accent Rule.** Text on an accent fill reads `--on-accent`; small text in the accent reads `--accent-text`. Lime, Gold, and Tape Orange are light accents, so their fills take the page color, the default (6.3:1 to 16:1); Terminal's green key takes Terminal Ground (7.45:1); Diorama's lamp takes its Ink (#17181b, 8.77:1), set as its `--on-accent`. Engineer sets `--on-accent` to white on Link Blue (6.70:1). Signal Orange measures 3.0:1 on Newsprint and 3.3:1 under white text, so Editorial sets `--on-accent` to Ink (5.2:1) and `--accent-text` to Signal Orange Ink (#bf3900, 4.5:1 or more on every Editorial surface); in Editorial, `.text-accent`, `hover:text-accent`, and `group-hover:text-accent` all render in it. Raw Signal Orange stays on rules, outlines, fills, and text at 24px and up, which opts in through `.text-accent-display`.

**The Reverse Video Rule (Engineer).** Engineer has two fills and no others: the blue Email key and reverse video (Ink fill, Cool Paper text). Reverse video marks what a pager would highlight: standalone figures in the copy (`.rfc-mark`, split out by `src/lib/figures.ts`, like `less` search matches), the pinned status line, and its "(END)".

**The Window Ink Rule (Tape).** Running copy sits in punched White windows in Tape Black ink. Orange never sets text inside a window (2.79:1); there it only underlines and rules. Orange text lives on the black ground.

**The Field Rule (Cartoon).** Every card names four colours: its field (`--field`), its text (`--on-field`), a safe colour for small text, focus rings, and hovers (`--safe`, 4.5:1 or more on the field), and a display colour for large type only (`--hi`). The card re-points `--text`, `--text-secondary`, `--accent`, `--accent-text`, `--on-accent`, `--border`, and Tailwind's `--color-*` utilities to them, so shared islands re-skin per card. Paper on Vermilion (3.4:1) is display only, never running text. A new card takes only one of the measured pairs.

**The Paper Card Rule (Diorama).** The room carries no running text. Everything a visitor reads sits on Paper in Ink (15.09:1), with Ink Soft for secondary copy: on wide screens the whole day is one paper page beside the room, the hero included; on phones and tablets each hour is a Paper card under the room's band, and only the hero copy (on the ground below the band), the clock, and the current hour's pin sit outside paper. Lamp Amber stays on the dark side (fills, pins, hovers); on paper its role passes to Lamp Ink (5.66:1): the margin's hours, the index's hours, the Email pill's hover, the underlines. The focus ring turns Ink, because the accent ring is too faint on paper.

### Named Rules
**The Heading Slot Rule.** Headings in shared components never hardcode a family. They use the heading slot (`--font-heading`) so a world can swap the voice in one token. A world's own stylesheet (`src/styles/worlds/<world>.css`) may name its faces directly, because those classes never render outside it.

**The Loud Display, Quiet Body Rule.** Identity lives in the display size and face. Body and label sizes stay between 0.75rem and 1.125rem in every world, except Tape's condensed body at 1.1875rem and its 1.25rem skill rows, which set the same measure a regular face sets at 1rem, and Cartoon's credit results at 1.1875rem.

**The One Size Rule (Engineer).** Engineer sets everything in Courier Prime at the text size except the hero's name and role. Hierarchy is numbering, position, a 3ch indent, and bold, never size.

**The Credits Rule (Cartoon).** A credit sets the name in the lettering over its function in small caps. A sub-credit adds its employer as a second small-caps line; a figure is billed the same way, the number as the name and what it counts beneath. Nothing labels a title from above.

**The Lettered Rule (Cartoon).** Display text is hand-lettered per letter (fixed tilt and rise patterns, offset by a seed per title so neighbours differ) and words never break inside. The letters are drawn for the eye and hidden from assistive tech; the whole text is read once from an `sr-only` span.

## Elevation & Depth

There is no system-wide elevation model; each world decides. Six of the eight convey depth with 1px borders and one step of surface tone (page, raised, card, card hover); Cartoon conveys it by overlap alone, Diorama by a real lit room beside a flat paper page. No world lifts its surfaces with shadow.

- **Engineer:** flat paper. Depth is typographic: indent, the 1px Ink box around a figure or table, reverse video, and the dashed form feed between pages. No shadow, no tint.
- **Avant-garde:** flat with glow. Borders in Charcoal Line, a 3% noise overlay for material, and a 16px Acid Lime glow at 12% on chip hover.
- **Editorial:** flat, hairline only. Depth comes from ghost numerals at 4% to 6% and hairlines that draw in on reveal.
- **Arcade:** flat and saturated. Candy surfaces sit directly on Cabinet Black with no border or shadow.
- **Terminal:** flat. Output that a terminal would box (JSON, the mail composer) sits in the raised panel with a 1px Terminal Line border; everything else is text on the ground.
- **Tape:** flat and cut, not stacked. Depth is material: White windows and the orange band sit on true black, and their punched holes (radial-gradient dots, SVG paths) show the ground through. An active career frame is inverted with a 6px White band along its foot (an inset edge, part of the mark, not a shadow).
- **Cartoon:** flat paper on paper. Each card's hand-cut top edge lies over the card above; the painter stands in front of his disc and over the band's end; the shorts are pasted at a tilt; at Contact the caricature stands behind the footer's seam. Inside the caricature, each colour is a flat plate printed a few units off register under the ink line. No gradient, no shadow, no blur inside the cards; hover answers with a print or a turn, not a lift.
- **Diorama:** the depth is the room's. A Three.js isometric miniature (a fixed elevation, an 18 degree field of view) lit by the hour: the sun through the right-hand window from 08:00 to evening, the sky's hemisphere light, and the lamps from 19:00, with soft PCF shadow maps (2048px, 1024px on phones, where the lamp casts none). On desktop a screen-space ambient occlusion pass (Three's GTAO, worked out at one pixel per CSS pixel and multiplied over the picture) darkens every place where things meet, the way a baked miniature does; phones keep the plain render. The model casts a soft shadow on the page under its plinth (desktop), so it stands on the ground instead of floating. On the wide shots (08:00, 22:00) the room is framed clear of the clock's plate: it slides left into any spare width, then gives up height from the top (at most a third) until its silhouette misses the plate by 14px. The walls are painted plaster, broad soft tone with no grain (the window wall a step lighter), so they never read as the carpet. The room is the only thing on the page that casts a shadow. The Paper cards over it are flat: no border, no shadow. The day's pins are Ink at 84% with a 1px Paper rim at 20%. The clock reads over the pale window at 13:00 and 16:00 on its own plate, a legibility device, not elevation.

Floating layers (the design dropdown, the mobile panel, blog card hover) use one soft shadow in every world.

### Shadow Vocabulary
- **Floating menu** (`box-shadow: 0 10px 15px -3px rgb(0 0 0 / 0.1), 0 4px 6px -4px rgb(0 0 0 / 0.1)`): the design dropdown in every world.
- **Mobile panel** (`box-shadow: 0 20px 25px -5px rgb(0 0 0 / 0.1), 0 8px 10px -6px rgb(0 0 0 / 0.1)`): the slide-in navigation.
- **Acid Lime glow** (`box-shadow: 0 0 16px rgba(197, 255, 65, 0.12)`): Avant-garde chip hover.
- **Clock plate** (Graphite at 70%, a 1px Paper edge at 8%, radius 0.16em, `0 0.05em 0.2em rgb(0 0 0 / 0.22)`): Diorama's clock only, so Paper digits hold over the lit window. It replaced a soft radial pool behind the digits (2026-11-02), which read as a smudge on the room.

### Named Rules
**The Paper on Paper Rule (Cartoon).** Depth in Cartoon comes only from one cut shape lying over another. A card, ticket, short, or figure never casts a shadow, takes a gradient, or blurs what is behind it; a new layer earns its place by overlapping.

**The Lit Room Rule (Diorama).** Light and shadow belong to the room and follow the hour. The page, cards, pins, and controls never take a shadow, a gradient, or a blur; depth is the room beside them.

## Shapes

Corner language is a per-world token pair (card radius, button radius) drawn from one shared scale.

- **Engineer:** 0px everywhere. Rectangles of text: 1px Ink borders on tables and figures, 2px dotted Rule Grey leaders, the 1px dashed form feed. No circles.
- **Avant-garde:** 8px cards, 4px buttons and chips. The 3px card edge and the 1px hero rule are its signature lines.
- **Editorial:** 0px everywhere. 2px outlined buttons, 1px hairlines, a 3px accent rule. Tech tags are underlines, not boxes.
- **Arcade:** 12px candy cards and full pills for buttons and chips.
- **Terminal:** 6px on keys, tags, and the output panel, the corner of a native terminal widget. The block cursor is a 0.62em by 1.15em solid rectangle.
- **Tape:** 0px everywhere, 2px strokes. Circles appear only as holes: data holes and feed holes in the band (14px pitch), a row of punched dots across the head of each window, feed holes down both edges of the Email key, and the 8px pins before each detail. State is a drawn mark in one 2px stroke: a cross for done, an inverted band for active, a folded flag for now.
- **Cartoon:** cut by hand. Every card's top edge is a wandering SVG mask, 24px tall on a 640px repeat (two cuts, alternated and offset so neighbours differ); rules are an 8px pen stroke in the card's colour; the role's band is a ragged roller mask. Tickets have unequal elliptical corners (6/14/8/16 over 14/6/16/8px) and a 3px stroke in the card's text colour (2.5px small, 4px on the Contact ticket); tags, the "Now showing" badge, and the Copy chip are cut smaller (3/8/4/9 over 8/3/9/4px), tags and the chip in a 2px stroke; shorts 5/12/6/14 over 12/5/14/6px with no stroke; bullets are irregular blobs. - **Diorama:** soft. - **Shared:** the portrait and social icon circles are full; the design dropdown, mobile menu links, and code blocks are 8px; the blog cover takes the world's card radius; inline code is 6px; the language toggle is 6px; the focus ring is 2px.

### Named Rules
**The Two-Radius Rule.** A world declares exactly two radii, `--radius-card` and `--radius-btn`. Chips, tags, and filters borrow the button radius; containers borrow the card radius.

**The Holes Are Data Rule (Tape).** Every punched strip encodes real text, one column per character in its ASCII bits (channel 1 at the bottom, the feed hole between channels 3 and 4), via `TapeStrip`. A blank leader carries feed holes only. Never draw random holes.

**The Hand-Cut Rule (Cartoon).** Inside the cards nothing is machine-true: edges, rules, tickets, tags, and bullets are cut or drawn, with unequal corners and wandering lines. The iris is the only true circle, because it is the camera's, not the paper's.

Terminal is the one curtain that holds: it ends on the frame its hero starts from and lifts the moment that hero has reset (2.2s at most).
- **Reduced motion:** CSS animations and transitions collapse to 0.01ms and smooth scrolling is disabled; Terminal's cursor stops blinking and its session renders finished; Cartoon's line stops boiling, the eyes stop blinking, the wave holds, its hero and titles are simply there, and the footer's iris rests closed; Diorama's camera jumps to the hour instead of easing, Akira's idle motion and the screens' blinking cursor stop, and the curtain's still and clock simply turn to 08:00. Motion's JavaScript animations are reached separately: `src/lib/reduced-motion.ts`, imported by a BaseLayout script, sets `MotionGlobalConfig.skipAnimations`, so every Motion animation jumps to its end state.
- **No JavaScript:** a `<noscript>` style in BaseLayout forces any element whose inline style starts at opacity 0 or `scaleX(0)` to full opacity with no transform, so Motion's server-rendered start states (including Editorial's rule) never hide the page. If a hero island throws, its ErrorBoundary renders a static fallback with the name, the role, the CV and the email instead of nothing.
- **Language on the server:** every island takes the page's `locale` as a prop (`withServerLocale`), so `/pt/`, `/pt/blog/` and Portuguese posts render in Portuguese before hydration and without JavaScript, in hidden worlds too; the logo, Home and Blog links stay in the page's language. Code-like strings (the draft's file name, shell commands, punched tape text) stay in English in both locales; the words they stand for are on the page.
- **Accessible names in animated type:** a display name split into per-letter spans for its entrance (Avant-garde) carries the whole name once in an `sr-only` span and hides the letters from assistive tech; word-split names (Editorial) keep real spaces between the words; Cartoon's hand-lettered titles, name, and credits do the same as Avant-garde; Diorama reads each chapter's hour and scene from visually hidden text before its heading, and a day pin whose scene name is clipped on screen keeps it for assistive tech. Decorative devices that repeat the page (Engineer's seams and pager, Terminal's prompt text in headings, every tape strip and mark, Cartoon's caricature, iris, and ampersand, Diorama's stage and clock) are hidden from assistive tech.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** declare a new world with the nine color tokens, two font slots, and two radii on its `[data-design]` selector (overriding `--on-accent` or `--accent-text` only if its accent misses 4.5:1, and adding world-only tokens only for its own material), register it in `src/data/designs.ts`, add its `design.<id>` name in both locales, and add it to the anti-FOUC allowlist and both visibility selector lists in `global.css`.
- **Do** keep the accent on buttons, labels, rules, and states (The One Accent Rule), and set text on an accent fill with `text-on-accent`, never a hardcoded white or page color.
- **Do** treat Signal Orange as a display color: rules, outlines, fills with Ink text, and text at 24px and up through `.text-accent-display`. Small orange text goes through `--accent-text` (Signal Orange Ink, #bf3900); raw Signal Orange measures 3.0:1 on Newsprint and 3.3:1 under white text.
- **Do** keep Tape Orange off text inside a White window (2.79:1); set window copy in Tape Black ink and let orange underline or rule there (The Window Ink Rule).
- **Do** resolve headings through the heading slot and body through the body slot in shared components; a world's own classes live in `src/styles/worlds/<world>.css` and may name its faces there.
- **Do** render both locales in every text node behind `data-lang-show` so the language toggle never re-renders.
- **Do** respect `prefers-reduced-motion`, and make new motion join the reveal grammar (fade, 30px rise, 0.5s, once) or the world's own signature.
- **Do** fork a shared section only when a world's identity needs it, inside the shared section with `data-design-hide` / `data-design-show` (The Same Bones Rule).
- **Do** carry state as a mark or a word as well as a colour (Tape's band and cross, Terminal's "●" and "Present", `aria-pressed`, `aria-expanded`).
- **Do** give a new Cartoon card a field, a text colour, a safe colour, and a display colour from the measured pairs (The Field Rule), and keep Paper on Vermilion to display type (3.4:1).
- **Do** animate Cartoon on held poses (`steps()`), never tweened, and keep its depth to overlap (The Paper on Paper Rule).
- **Do** mount a WebGL scene only while its world is active, calibrate a lighter phone build, and ship a fallback that keeps the page whole, as Avant-garde (CSS dots) and Diorama (the 08:00 still and a list of hours) do.
- **Do** put everything a Diorama visitor reads on paper in Ink (the day's page on wide screens, a card per hour on phones), keep Lamp Amber on the dark side, and use Lamp Ink on paper (The Paper Card Rule).
- **Do** make a new Diorama section a chapter (`DioramaChapter`, one station in `day.ts`, one row of the dimming list in `diorama.css`) with its hour in the margin, and add its hour to the day's index (The Clock Rule).

### Don't:
- **Don't** mix tokens across worlds. No candy surface in Engineer, no prompt green outside Terminal, no Tape Orange outside Tape, no Cartoon field outside Cartoon, no Diorama lamp or paper card outside Diorama (The Own Palette Rule).
- **Don't** fork content per world. Copy, data, order, and links come from `src/data` and `src/i18n` only; a world may restage them, never rewrite or reorder them.
- **Don't** add a fill to Engineer beyond the Email key and reverse video, or a size beyond the text size and the hero title (The Reverse Video Rule, The One Size Rule).
- **Don't** punch decorative noise into Tape: every strip encodes its text (The Holes Are Data Rule).
- **Don't** give a Cartoon card a shadow, a gradient, a blur, a frame, halftone dots, or comic-book lettering effects: it is cut paper, not a comic page or a neo-brutalist card. Strokes stay drawn lines on tickets and tags in the card's own colour.
- **Don't** label a Cartoon title from above; a credit is the name over its function in small caps (The Credits Rule).
- **Don't** print an hour kicker or eyebrow above a Diorama heading (the hour goes in the margin, beside it), or let the page reach into the room's panel (The Clock Rule).
- **Don't** give the Diorama page, a card, pin, or control a shadow, and don't set running text over the room (The Lit Room Rule, The Paper Card Rule).
- **Don't** turn Diorama's room into a click-to-explore scene or a first-person walkthrough: scroll is the clock.
