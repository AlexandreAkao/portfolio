# Design System: Alexandre Akira Portfolio

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Repertoire"**

One performer, seven staged productions of the same script. The content (name, title, tagline, experience, skills, projects, blog) never changes; a switch in the header restages it in one of seven complete visual worlds: Editorial (the default), Avant-garde, Engineer, Creative, Arcade, Terminal, and Tape. The system's personality is plural on purpose. Each world commits fully to its own register (the broadsheet, the dark gallery, the Internet-Draft read in a pager, the studio, the arcade cabinet, the shell session, and the punched teletype tape). None of them is a theme laid over a neutral base, because the neutral base does not exist.

Underneath, one skeleton carries all seven: a fixed translucent header, a full-viewport hero, and a single centered column of sections (About, Experience, Education, Skills, Projects, Contact) at medium density. A world declares nine semantic color tokens (page, raised, text, secondary text, accent, accent hover, border, card, card hover), two font slots, and two radii, and the shared sections re-skin themselves. Two accent-use tokens sit on top for every world: `--on-accent`, the text on an accent fill (the page color by default), and `--accent-text`, small text in the accent (the accent itself by default); a world overrides either only when its accent needs it, as Editorial and Engineer do. A world may add tokens of its own beside the nine when its material needs them (Terminal's prompt green and bright text, Tape's punched windows); they never leave that world. Every world stages its own Hero, Experience, and Skills; Editorial, Engineer, Terminal, and Tape also stage their own About and footer, and the last three their own Education, Projects, and Contact.

The aesthetic philosophy is restraint everywhere except the accent and the display type. Every world spends its accent on small, high-signal things: buttons, period labels, rules, hover and selection states. Display typography carries identity, except in Engineer, where one face at one size carries it and hierarchy comes from numbering, position, indent, and bold. Motion is one shared reveal grammar (fade and rise on scroll, once) plus a single signature per world, staged in the hero and in its curtain.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Seven complete worlds, one content source, switched at runtime and persisted per visitor.
- One accent per world, spent on interactive and labeling elements only; Terminal's prompt green is its one sanctioned second voice.
- Display typography carries identity: monospace, typewriter, geometric sans, heavy grotesk, display grotesk, slab poster, or condensed caps.
- Flat, border-defined surfaces in six worlds; Creative alone lifts with shadow.
- Shared reveal motion (fade, 30px rise, 0.5s, once) with a signature per world, staged in the hero.
- WebGL in one world only (Avant-garde's particle field), mounted only while that world is active and backed by a CSS fallback.
- Candy stays on the cards: Arcade's candy colors are surfaces, never UI chrome.
- Bilingual by construction: every text node renders both locales and CSS shows one.

### Named Rules
**The One Accent Rule.** Each world has exactly one accent and spends it on buttons, labels, rules, hover and selection states. Body copy, headings, and card surfaces never take the accent. Arcade's candy colors are surfaces with Cabinet Black text on them, never accents. Terminal's prompt green is the shell's own prompt colour, held to the prompt user, active periods, JSON values, and the email key; it never becomes a second link or hover colour.

**The Own Palette Rule.** A world uses only its own token set, world-only tokens included. No Candy Green in Engineer, no Creative shadow in Editorial, no Tape Orange in Editorial or Signal Orange in Tape, no prompt green outside the terminal.

**The Dark Text on Bright Accent Rule.** Text on an accent fill reads `--on-accent`; small text in the accent reads `--accent-text`. Lime, Gold, and Tape Orange are light accents, so their fills take the page color, the default (6.3:1 to 16:1); Terminal's green key takes Terminal Ground (7.45:1). Violet's own hero and filter fills take white (6.7:1), and Engineer sets `--on-accent` to white on Link Blue (6.70:1). Signal Orange measures 3.0:1 on Newsprint and 3.3:1 under white text, so Editorial sets `--on-accent` to Ink (5.2:1) and `--accent-text` to Signal Orange Ink (#bf3900, 4.5:1 or more on every Editorial surface); in Editorial, `.text-accent`, `hover:text-accent`, and `group-hover:text-accent` all render in it. Raw Signal Orange stays on rules, outlines, fills, and text at 24px and up, which opts in through `.text-accent-display`.

**The Reverse Video Rule (Engineer).** Engineer has two fills and no others: the blue Email key and reverse video (Ink fill, Cool Paper text). Reverse video marks what a pager would highlight: standalone figures in the copy (`.rfc-mark`, split out by `src/lib/figures.ts`, like `less` search matches), the pinned status line, and its "(END)".

**The Window Ink Rule (Tape).** Running copy sits in punched White windows in Tape Black ink. Orange never sets text inside a window (2.79:1); there it only underlines and rules. Orange text lives on the black ground.

## Typography

**Display Font:** per world. Engineer: Courier Prime (monospace). Creative: Sora (sans-serif). Avant-garde: Poppins (sans-serif). Editorial: Clash Display (sans-serif, served from Fontshare). Arcade: Bungee (sans-serif, single weight). Terminal: JetBrains Mono (monospace). Tape: Big Shoulders Display (condensed sans-serif, 800 and 900).
**Body Font:** per world. Engineer: Courier Prime (monospace, the same face). Creative: Source Serif 4 (serif). Avant-garde: Inter (sans-serif). Editorial: DM Sans Variable (sans-serif). Arcade: Outfit (sans-serif). Terminal: JetBrains Mono (monospace, the same face). Tape: Big Shoulders Text (condensed sans-serif, 500 to 800).
**Label/Mono Font:** JetBrains Mono (monospace) for the design switcher label, inline code, and code blocks in every world.

**Character:** The display slot carries each world's identity. Courier Prime reads as a typed specification, Sora as a rounded modern studio, Poppins as a heavy geometric gallery wall, Clash Display as a broadsheet masthead, Bungee as a marquee sign, JetBrains Mono as a shell, Big Shoulders as stencilled machine caps. Body faces stay quiet and legible so the display can be loud; Engineer and Terminal set heading and body in one face on purpose. Every heading in a shared section resolves through the heading slot, so re-skinning changes the voice without touching a component.

### Named Rules
**The Heading Slot Rule.** Headings in shared components never hardcode a family. They use the heading slot (`--font-heading`) so a world can swap the voice in one token. A world's own stylesheet (`src/styles/worlds/<world>.css`) may name its faces directly, because those classes never render outside it.

**The Loud Display, Quiet Body Rule.** Identity lives in the display size and face. Body and label sizes stay between 0.75rem and 1.125rem in every world, except Tape's condensed body at 1.1875rem and its 1.25rem skill rows, which set the same measure a regular face sets at 1rem.

**The One Size Rule (Engineer).** Engineer sets everything in Courier Prime at the text size except the hero's name and role. Hierarchy is numbering, position, a 3ch indent, and bold, never size.

## Elevation & Depth

There is no system-wide elevation model; each world decides. Six of the seven convey depth with 1px borders and one step of surface tone (page, raised, card, card hover). Creative is the only world that lifts.

- **Engineer:** flat paper. Depth is typographic: indent, the 1px Ink box around a figure or table, reverse video, and the dashed form feed between pages. No shadow, no tint.
- **Creative:** lifted. Cards carry a soft ambient shadow, the portrait a deep one, chips a hairline shadow that grows on hover. Cards and chips also rise 4px on hover.
- **Avant-garde:** flat with glow. Borders in Charcoal Line, a 3% noise overlay for material, and a 16px Acid Lime glow at 12% on chip hover.
- **Editorial:** flat, hairline only. Depth comes from ghost numerals at 4% to 6% and hairlines that draw in on reveal.
- **Arcade:** flat and saturated. Candy surfaces sit directly on Cabinet Black with no border or shadow.
- **Terminal:** flat. Output that a terminal would box (JSON, the mail composer) sits in the raised panel with a 1px Terminal Line border; everything else is text on the ground.
- **Tape:** flat and cut, not stacked. Depth is material: White windows and the orange band sit on true black, and their punched holes (radial-gradient dots, SVG paths) show the ground through. An active career frame is inverted with a 6px White band along its foot (an inset edge, part of the mark, not a shadow).

Floating layers (the design dropdown, the mobile panel, blog card hover) use one soft shadow in every world.

## Shapes

Corner language is a per-world token pair (card radius, button radius) drawn from one shared scale.

- **Engineer:** 0px everywhere. Rectangles of text: 1px Ink borders on tables and figures, 2px dotted Rule Grey leaders, the 1px dashed form feed. No circles.
- **Creative:** 16px cards and full pills for buttons, chips, tabs, and badges. Concentric circles around the portrait.
- **Avant-garde:** 8px cards, 4px buttons and chips. The 3px card edge and the 1px hero rule are its signature lines.
- **Editorial:** 0px everywhere. 2px outlined buttons, 1px hairlines, a 3px accent rule. Tech tags are underlines, not boxes.
- **Arcade:** 12px candy cards and full pills for buttons and chips.
- **Terminal:** 6px on keys, tags, and the output panel, the corner of a native terminal widget. The block cursor is a 0.62em by 1.15em solid rectangle.
- **Tape:** 0px everywhere, 2px strokes. Circles appear only as holes: data holes and feed holes in the band (14px pitch), a row of punched dots across the head of each window, feed holes down both edges of the Email key, and the 8px pins before each detail. State is a drawn mark in one 2px stroke: a cross for done, an inverted band for active, a folded flag for now.
- **Shared:** the portrait and social icon circles are full; the design dropdown, mobile menu links, and code blocks are 8px; the blog cover takes the world's card radius; inline code is 6px; the language toggle is 6px; the focus ring is 2px.

### Named Rules
**The Two-Radius Rule.** A world declares exactly two radii, `--radius-card` and `--radius-btn`. Chips, tags, and filters borrow the button radius; containers borrow the card radius.

**The Holes Are Data Rule (Tape).** Every punched strip encodes real text, one column per character in its ASCII bits (channel 1 at the bottom, the feed hole between channels 3 and 4), via `TapeStrip`. A blank leader carries feed holes only. Never draw random holes.

### Motion
- **Reveal:** every section and card fades in and rises 30px over 0.5s with ease-out, once, when it is 50px into view. Siblings stagger by 0.1s, or 0.03s to 0.05s for chips and characters.
- **World switch:** the quick-change curtain, 0.6s to 0.8s, staged as the incoming world's exit gesture; a 0.3s View Transition crossfade under reduced motion or as fallback.
- **Hover:** color transitions at 150ms. Creative scales buttons to 1.05 and lifts chips 4px; cards lift 4px.
- **Menus:** the dropdown fades and scales from 0.95 over 0.15s; the mobile panel slides over 0.3s behind a 0.2s backdrop.
- **Hero signatures:** Avant-garde's 0.03s character stagger and a rule that scales in over 0.8s; Editorial's 0.15s word stagger on the name with parallax (name -80px, subtitle -40px across the hero's scroll), while the rule, role, employer line, tagline and actions follow within 0.15–0.5s so the role is legible in about 0.7s; Creative's portrait scaling from 0.8 over 0.8s. Engineer prints its first page top to bottom, each block appearing outright 45ms after the last, like a pager drawing a screen (no fade, no rise). Terminal replays its session. Tape's window rises 24px over 0.5s, the right column and tagline fade in at 0.25s and 0.35s, the band feeds from 35% over 0.9s, and the flag drops at 0.8s; under the intro curtain the window and the band are already in place and the flag drops once the curtain has lifted.
- **Intro curtain:** 1.3s to 1.9s per world, fonts awaited up to 700ms first; heroes hydrate when the exit begins (0.95s to 1.35s) so the curtain opens on a hero mid-entrance. Terminal is the one curtain that holds: it ends on the frame its hero starts from and lifts the moment that hero has reset (2.2s at most).
- **Reduced motion:** CSS animations and transitions collapse to 0.01ms and smooth scrolling is disabled; Terminal's cursor stops blinking and its session renders finished. Motion's JavaScript animations are reached separately: `src/lib/reduced-motion.ts`, imported by a BaseLayout script, sets `MotionGlobalConfig.skipAnimations`, so every Motion animation jumps to its end state.
- **No JavaScript:** a `<noscript>` style in BaseLayout forces any element whose inline style starts at opacity 0 or `scaleX(0)` to full opacity with no transform, so Motion's server-rendered start states (including Editorial's rule) never hide the page. If a hero island throws, its ErrorBoundary renders a static fallback with the name, the role, the CV and the email instead of nothing.
- **Language on the server:** every island takes the page's `locale` as a prop (`withServerLocale`), so `/pt/`, `/pt/blog/` and Portuguese posts render in Portuguese before hydration and without JavaScript, in hidden worlds too; the logo, Home and Blog links stay in the page's language. Code-like strings (the draft's file name, shell commands, punched tape text) stay in English in both locales; the words they stand for are on the page.
- **Accessible names in animated type:** a display name split into per-letter spans for its entrance (Avant-garde) carries the whole name once in an `sr-only` span and hides the letters from assistive tech; word-split names (Editorial) keep real spaces between the words. Decorative devices that repeat the page (Engineer's seams and pager, Terminal's prompt text in headings, every tape strip and mark) are hidden from assistive tech.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** declare a new world with the nine color tokens, two font slots, and two radii on its `[data-design]` selector (overriding `--on-accent` or `--accent-text` only if its accent misses 4.5:1, and adding world-only tokens only for its own material), register it in `src/data/designs.ts`, add its `design.<id>` name in both locales, and add it to the anti-FOUC allowlist and both visibility selector lists in `global.css`.
- **Do** keep the accent on buttons, labels, rules, and states (The One Accent Rule), and set text on an accent fill with `text-on-accent`, never a hardcoded white or page color.
- **Do** treat Signal Orange as a display color: rules, outlines, fills with Ink text, and text at 24px and up through `.text-accent-display`. Small orange text goes through `--accent-text` (Signal Orange Ink, #bf3900); raw Signal Orange measures 3.0:1 on Newsprint and 3.3:1 under white text.
- **Do** keep Tape Orange off text inside a White window (2.79:1); set window copy in Tape Black ink and let orange underline or rule there (The Window Ink Rule).
- **Do** resolve headings through the heading slot and body through the body slot in shared components; a world's own classes live in `src/styles/worlds/<world>.css` and may name its faces there.
- **Do** render both locales in every text node behind `data-lang-show` so the language toggle never re-renders.
- **Do** respect `prefers-reduced-motion`, and make new motion join the reveal grammar (fade, 30px rise, 0.5s, once) or the world's own signature.
- **Do** fork a shared section only when a world's identity needs it, inside the shared section with `data-design-hide` / `data-design-show` (The Same Bones Rule).
- **Do** carry state as a mark or a word as well as a colour (Tape's band and cross, Terminal's "●" and "Present", `aria-pressed`, `aria-expanded`).
- **Do** mount a WebGL scene only while its world is active, calibrate a lighter phone build, and ship a CSS fallback, as Avant-garde does.

### Don't:
- **Don't** mix tokens across worlds. No candy surface in Engineer, no Creative shadow in Editorial, no prompt green outside Terminal, no Tape Orange outside Tape (The Own Palette Rule).
- **Don't** fork content per world. Copy, data, order, and links come from `src/data` and `src/i18n` only; a world may restage them, never rewrite or reorder them.
- **Don't** add a fill to Engineer beyond the Email key and reverse video, or a size beyond the text size and the hero title (The Reverse Video Rule, The One Size Rule).
- **Don't** punch decorative noise into Tape: every strip encodes its text (The Holes Are Data Rule).
