# Design System: Alexandre Akira Portfolio

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Repertoire"**

One performer, nine staged productions of the same script. The content (name, title, tagline, experience, skills, projects, blog) never changes; a switch in the header restages it in one of nine complete visual worlds: Editorial (the default), Avant-garde, Engineer, Creative, Arcade, Terminal, Tape, Cartoon, and Clay. The system's personality is plural on purpose. Each world commits fully to its own register (the broadsheet, the dark gallery, the Internet-Draft read in a pager, the studio, the arcade cabinet, the shell session, the punched teletype tape, the opening titles of a 1960s cartoon, and a developer's desk moulded in plasticine). None of them is a theme laid over a neutral base, because the neutral base does not exist.

Underneath, one skeleton carries all nine: a fixed translucent header, a full-viewport hero, and a single centered column of sections (About, Experience, Education, Skills, Projects, Contact) at medium density. A world declares nine semantic color tokens (page, raised, text, secondary text, accent, accent hover, border, card, card hover), two font slots, and two radii, and the shared sections re-skin themselves. Two accent-use tokens sit on top for every world: `--on-accent`, the text on an accent fill (the page color by default), and `--accent-text`, small text in the accent (the accent itself by default); a world overrides either only when its accent needs it, as Editorial and Engineer do. A world may add tokens of its own beside the nine when its material needs them (Terminal's prompt green and bright text, Tape's punched windows, Cartoon's five paper fields, Clay's five clays and its surface tile); they never leave that world. Every world stages its own Hero, Experience, and Skills; Editorial, Engineer, Terminal, Tape, Cartoon, and Clay also stage their own About and footer, and the last five their own Education, Projects, and Contact.

The aesthetic philosophy is restraint everywhere except the accent and the display type. Every world spends its accent on small, high-signal things: buttons, period labels, rules, hover and selection states. Cartoon turns the strategy over: colour is the field, not the accent; every section is a title card drenched in one of five flat paper colours, and on each card the accent is the one colour that card names safe for small text. Clay keeps one field, the painted teal sweep, and puts its colour in the clay laid on it; its pink accent is only ever a fill with ink on it, never text. Display typography carries identity, except in Engineer, where one face at one size carries it and hierarchy comes from numbering, position, indent, and bold. Motion is one shared reveal grammar (fade and rise on scroll, once) plus a single signature per world, staged in the hero and in its curtain; Cartoon and Clay replace the tween with poses held on twos.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Nine complete worlds, one content source, switched at runtime and persisted per visitor.
- One accent per world, spent on interactive and labeling elements only; Terminal's prompt green is its one sanctioned second voice.
- Display typography carries identity: monospace, typewriter, geometric sans, heavy grotesk, display grotesk, slab poster, condensed caps, hand-lettered cartoon titling, or letters rolled from clay.
- Flat surfaces in seven worlds: border-defined in six, drenched cut-paper fields in Cartoon, where depth is only paper lying on paper. Two worlds lift: Creative with a soft neutral shadow, Clay with lit, shaded plasticine that casts a soft teal shadow on its sweep.
- Shared reveal motion (fade, 30px rise, 0.5s, once) with a signature per world, staged in the hero.
- WebGL in two worlds: Avant-garde's particle field, mounted only while that world is active and backed by a CSS fallback, and Clay's live tabletop set, likewise mounted only while Clay is active, rendered at 12 frames a second on demand and paused off screen.
- Candy stays on the cards: Arcade's candy colors are surfaces, never UI chrome. Cartoon's fields are surfaces too, one per section; Clay's clays are material, laid on the sweep.
- Limited animation in Cartoon and Clay: poses held on twos (`steps()`), never tweened.
- Bilingual by construction: every text node renders both locales and CSS shows one.

### Named Rules
**The One Accent Rule.** Each world has exactly one accent and spends it on buttons, labels, rules, hover and selection states. Body copy, headings, and card surfaces never take the accent. Arcade's candy colors are surfaces with Cabinet Black text on them, never accents. Terminal's prompt green is the shell's own prompt colour, held to the prompt user, active periods, JSON values, and the email key; it never becomes a second link or hover colour. Cartoon's fields are surfaces, like the candy; on each card the accent is the one safe colour that card names.

**The Own Palette Rule.** A world uses only its own token set, world-only tokens included. No Candy Green in Engineer, no Creative shadow in Editorial, no Tape Orange in Editorial or Signal Orange in Tape, no prompt green outside the terminal, no Cartoon field outside Cartoon, no clay outside Clay.

**The Dark Text on Bright Accent Rule.** Text on an accent fill reads `--on-accent`; small text in the accent reads `--accent-text`. Lime, Gold, and Tape Orange are light accents, so their fills take the page color, the default (6.3:1 to 16:1); Terminal's green key takes Terminal Ground (7.45:1). Violet's own hero and filter fills take white (6.7:1), and Engineer sets `--on-accent` to white on Link Blue (6.70:1). Signal Orange measures 3.0:1 on Newsprint and 3.3:1 under white text, so Editorial sets `--on-accent` to Ink (5.2:1) and `--accent-text` to Signal Orange Ink (#bf3900, 4.5:1 or more on every Editorial surface); in Editorial, `.text-accent`, `hover:text-accent`, and `group-hover:text-accent` all render in it. Raw Signal Orange stays on rules, outlines, fills, and text at 24px and up, which opts in through `.text-accent-display`. Clay Pink fails even display size on the sweep (1.02:1), so Clay sets both tokens and `.text-accent-display` to Clay Ink.

**The Reverse Video Rule (Engineer).** Engineer has two fills and no others: the blue Email key and reverse video (Ink fill, Cool Paper text). Reverse video marks what a pager would highlight: standalone figures in the copy (`.rfc-mark`, split out by `src/lib/figures.ts`, like `less` search matches), the pinned status line, and its "(END)".

**The Window Ink Rule (Tape).** Running copy sits in punched White windows in Tape Black ink. Orange never sets text inside a window (2.79:1); there it only underlines and rules. Orange text lives on the black ground.

**The Field Rule (Cartoon).** Every card names four colours: its field (`--field`), its text (`--on-field`), a safe colour for small text, focus rings, and hovers (`--safe`, 4.5:1 or more on the field), and a display colour for large type only (`--hi`). The card re-points `--text`, `--text-secondary`, `--accent`, `--accent-text`, `--on-accent`, `--border`, and Tailwind's `--color-*` utilities to them, so shared islands re-skin per card. Paper on Vermilion (3.4:1) is display only, never running text. A new card takes only one of the measured pairs.

**The Ink on Clay Rule (Clay).** Colour in Clay is material, not type. Every clay that carries words carries Clay Ink (6.07:1 to 15.8:1); the only light text is on ink clay (the FPS meter) and on the footer's Wrap. Pink is the accent as a fill, an underline, a bead, or a coil, never as running text; Purple and the side-wall shades carry no words at all. A new clay surface takes ink, or takes no text.

## Typography

**Display Font:** per world. Engineer: Courier Prime (monospace). Creative: Sora (sans-serif). Avant-garde: Poppins (sans-serif). Editorial: Clash Display (sans-serif, served from Fontshare). Arcade: Bungee (sans-serif, single weight). Terminal: JetBrains Mono (monospace). Tape: Big Shoulders Display (condensed sans-serif, 800 and 900). Cartoon: Londrina Solid (hand-lettered sans-serif, 900). Clay: Bagel Fat One (rolled-clay display sans-serif, single weight 400, self-hosted via `@fontsource/bagel-fat-one`).
**Body Font:** per world. Engineer: Courier Prime (monospace, the same face). Creative: Source Serif 4 (serif). Avant-garde: Inter (sans-serif). Editorial: DM Sans Variable (sans-serif). Arcade: Outfit (sans-serif). Terminal: JetBrains Mono (monospace, the same face). Tape: Big Shoulders Text (condensed sans-serif, 500 to 800). Cartoon: Jost Variable (geometric sans-serif, 400 to 700). Clay: Gabarito Variable (geometric sans-serif, 400 to 800, via `@fontsource-variable/gabarito`).
**Label/Mono Font:** JetBrains Mono (monospace) for the design switcher label, inline code, and code blocks in every world.

**Character:** The display slot carries each world's identity. Courier Prime reads as a typed specification, Sora as a rounded modern studio, Poppins as a heavy geometric gallery wall, Clash Display as a broadsheet masthead, Bungee as a marquee sign, JetBrains Mono as a shell, Big Shoulders as stencilled machine caps, Londrina Solid as a title artist's hand lettering, Bagel Fat One as letters rolled from plasticine, written as code (tags in brackets). Body faces stay quiet and legible so the display can be loud; Engineer and Terminal set heading and body in one face on purpose. Every heading in a shared section resolves through the heading slot, so re-skinning changes the voice without touching a component.

### Named Rules
**The Heading Slot Rule.** Headings in shared components never hardcode a family. They use the heading slot (`--font-heading`) so a world can swap the voice in one token. A world's own stylesheet (`src/styles/worlds/<world>.css`) may name its faces directly, because those classes never render outside it.

**The Loud Display, Quiet Body Rule.** Identity lives in the display size and face. Body and label sizes stay between 0.75rem and 1.125rem in every world, except Tape's condensed body at 1.1875rem and its 1.25rem skill rows, which set the same measure a regular face sets at 1rem, Cartoon's credit results at 1.1875rem, and Clay's running copy at 1.0625rem, its lede at 1.25rem.

**The One Size Rule (Engineer).** Engineer sets everything in Courier Prime at the text size except the hero's name and role. Hierarchy is numbering, position, a 3ch indent, and bold, never size.

**The Credits Rule (Cartoon).** A credit sets the name in the lettering over its function in small caps. A sub-credit adds its employer as a second small-caps line; a figure is billed the same way, the number as the name and what it counts beneath. Nothing labels a title from above.

**The Lettered Rule (Cartoon).** Display text is hand-lettered per letter (fixed tilt and rise patterns, offset by a seed per title so neighbours differ) and words never break inside. The letters are drawn for the eye and hidden from assistive tech; the whole text is read once from an `sr-only` span.

## Elevation & Depth

There is no system-wide elevation model; each world decides. Six of the nine convey depth with 1px borders and one step of surface tone (page, raised, card, card hover); Cartoon conveys it by overlap alone. Creative and Clay lift.

- **Engineer:** flat paper. Depth is typographic: indent, the 1px Ink box around a figure or table, reverse video, and the dashed form feed between pages. No shadow, no tint.
- **Creative:** lifted. Cards carry a soft ambient shadow, the portrait a deep one, chips a hairline shadow that grows on hover. Cards and chips also rise 4px on hover.
- **Avant-garde:** flat with glow. Borders in Charcoal Line, a 3% noise overlay for material, and a 16px Acid Lime glow at 12% on chip hover.
- **Editorial:** flat, hairline only. Depth comes from ghost numerals at 4% to 6% and hairlines that draw in on reveal.
- **Arcade:** flat and saturated. Candy surfaces sit directly on Cabinet Black with no border or shadow.
- **Terminal:** flat. Output that a terminal would box (JSON, the mail composer) sits in the raised panel with a 1px Terminal Line border; everything else is text on the ground.
- **Tape:** flat and cut, not stacked. Depth is material: White windows and the orange band sit on true black, and their punched holes (radial-gradient dots, SVG paths) show the ground through. An active career frame is inverted with a 6px White band along its foot (an inset edge, part of the mark, not a shadow).
- **Cartoon:** flat paper on paper. Each card's hand-cut top edge lies over the card above; the painter stands in front of his disc and over the band's end; the shorts are pasted at a tilt; at Contact the caricature stands behind the footer's seam. Inside the caricature, each colour is a flat plate printed a few units off register under the ink line. No gradient, no shadow, no blur inside the cards; hover answers with a print or a turn, not a lift.
- **Clay:** lit plasticine on a tabletop. Every clay surface is its colour with the surface tile (`clay-surface.webp`, a mid-grey tile of thumb presses, fingerprints, and tool marks) pressed in under `background-blend-mode: hard-light`, a lit top edge and a shaded underside as insets (`--clay-lift`), and one soft shadow cast on the sweep in the sweep's own dark teal (`--clay-cast`), never a hard offset. Each surface reads its own patch of the tile: `--tx` and `--ty` move it and `--ts` sizes it (320px by default), set per index by the components so neighbours never share fingerprints. Keycaps are two layers: the face, and beneath it a side wall in the same clay's shade set `--depth` lower (4 to 9px), which casts the shadow. Coils (the branch, the title underlines, the curtain's `</>`) are lit down one side and shaded down the other. In the hero the depth is real: a lit Three.js set with soft percentage-closer shadows.

Floating layers (the design dropdown, the mobile panel, blog card hover) use one soft shadow in every world.

### Named Rules
**The Paper on Paper Rule (Cartoon).** Depth in Cartoon comes only from one cut shape lying over another. A card, ticket, short, or figure never casts a shadow, takes a gradient, or blurs what is behind it; a new layer earns its place by overlapping.

**The Lit Clay Rule (Clay).** A clay surface is always the full recipe: its clay colour, the surface tile under hard-light, the lift insets, and a soft cast in the sweep's teal. Never a flat clay, a black shadow, or a hard offset shadow; never a gradient standing in for the light.

### Named Rules
**The Two-Radius Rule.** A world declares exactly two radii, `--radius-card` and `--radius-btn`. Chips, tags, and filters borrow the button radius; containers borrow the card radius.

**The Holes Are Data Rule (Tape).** Every punched strip encodes real text, one column per character in its ASCII bits (channel 1 at the bottom, the feed hole between channels 3 and 4), via `TapeStrip`. A blank leader carries feed holes only. Never draw random holes.

**The Hand-Cut Rule (Cartoon).** Inside the cards nothing is machine-true: edges, rules, tickets, tags, and bullets are cut or drawn, with unequal corners and wandering lines. The iris is the only true circle, because it is the camera's, not the paper's.

**The Pressed by Hand Rule (Clay).** No clay surface has four equal corners or sits dead level. Slabs, windows, keycaps, notes, and folders take unequal radii, and slabs and notes a small tilt; only rolled pieces (beads, coils, commits, pills) are round.

Every raster has a provenance sidecar (`*.webp.json`) and its dev-only generator in `src/components/designs/clay/render/`. Decorative and hidden from assistive tech.
- **Quick-change (theme switch):** the same curtain staged short. On a switch the curtain wears the incoming world's tokens, fades in over 0.15s, the theme swaps underneath, and only that world's exit gesture plays: the stage splits (Avant-garde), the draft pages back from (END) to page 1 in 0.3s (Engineer), the halo glides to the portrait (Creative), the sheet turns (Editorial), the candy bars sweep out (Arcade), the ssh transcript prints already typed and lifts onto the hero's first prompt at 0.45s, or once a first-time hero has reset (Terminal), the name already printed, the reader runs the role in an 11ms-a-column burst and the curtain fades (Tape), the iris opens on the incoming painter from 0.2s to 0.7s with the painter and his disc already drawn (Cartoon, 0.75s), the `</>` already rolled out (no name) is squashed and lifted away from 0.12s (Clay, 0.7s). No title card, no name. 0.6s to 0.8s in total. Reduced motion, a curtain already running, or an unknown world fall back to the 0.3s crossfade.

### Motion
- **Reveal:** every section and card fades in and rises 30px over 0.5s with ease-out, once, when it is 50px into view. Siblings stagger by 0.1s, or 0.03s to 0.05s for chips and characters.
- **World switch:** the quick-change curtain, 0.6s to 0.8s, staged as the incoming world's exit gesture; a 0.3s View Transition crossfade under reduced motion or as fallback.
- **Hover:** color transitions at 150ms. Creative scales buttons to 1.05 and lifts chips 4px; cards lift 4px.
- **Menus:** the dropdown fades and scales from 0.95 over 0.15s; the mobile panel slides over 0.3s behind a 0.2s backdrop.
- **Hero signatures:** Avant-garde's 0.03s character stagger and a rule that scales in over 0.8s; Editorial's 0.15s word stagger on the name with parallax (name -80px, subtitle -40px across the hero's scroll), while the rule, role, employer line, tagline and actions follow within 0.15–0.5s so the role is legible in about 0.7s; Creative's portrait scaling from 0.8 over 0.8s. Engineer prints its first page top to bottom, each block appearing outright 45ms after the last, like a pager drawing a screen (no fade, no rise). Terminal replays its session. Tape's window rises 24px over 0.5s, the right column and tagline fade in at 0.25s and 0.35s, the band feeds from 35% over 0.9s, and the flag drops at 0.8s; under the intro curtain the window and the band are already in place and the flag drops once the curtain has lifted. Cartoon plays limited animation, poses held on twos (`steps()`), never tweened: the disc pops at 0.08s, the painter at 0.15s (small, overshoot, settle), the name's letters drop in 45ms apart from 0.1s, the band is rolled on in six steps from 0.45s, and the rest steps in from 0.3s; under the curtain the painter is already on his mark and the rest waits for the iris. Section titles drop in letter by letter on a view timeline as the card scrolls in, then the caricature pops up over the title; Education's blackboard chalks up each year in turn as it scrolls in (a five-step wipe on the view timeline). Clay is stop-motion: the set's canvas comes up in two steps (0.25s, the studio lights), then the copy pops in line by line from 0.05s to 0.5s, each a squash and settle held in three poses (`steps(1)`, 0.34s), paused while the curtain is up; each editor window plops into place (a squash held in three poses, `steps(1)`, 0.34s) when a quarter of it is on screen, and is never hidden first; keycaps sink on `steps(2)`, folders squash on `steps(3)`, a running commit pulses on `steps(1)`.
- **Intro curtain:** 1.3s to 1.9s per world, fonts awaited up to 700ms first; heroes hydrate when the exit begins (0.95s to 1.35s) so the curtain opens on a hero mid-entrance. Clay's runs 1.2s. Terminal is the one curtain that holds: it ends on the frame its hero starts from and lifts the moment that hero has reset (2.2s at most).
- **Reduced motion:** CSS animations and transitions collapse to 0.01ms and smooth scrolling is disabled; Terminal's cursor stops blinking and its session renders finished; Cartoon's line stops boiling, the eyes stop blinking, the wave holds, its hero and titles are simply there, and the footer's iris rests closed; Clay's set renders one still frame, the stills hold their first frame, and the windows and the running commit hold still. Motion's JavaScript animations are reached separately: `src/lib/reduced-motion.ts`, imported by a BaseLayout script, sets `MotionGlobalConfig.skipAnimations`, so every Motion animation jumps to its end state.
- **No JavaScript:** a `<noscript>` style in BaseLayout forces any element whose inline style starts at opacity 0 or `scaleX(0)` to full opacity with no transform, so Motion's server-rendered start states (including Editorial's rule) never hide the page. If a hero island throws, its ErrorBoundary renders a static fallback with the name, the role, the CV and the email instead of nothing.
- **Language on the server:** every island takes the page's `locale` as a prop (`withServerLocale`), so `/pt/`, `/pt/blog/` and Portuguese posts render in Portuguese before hydration and without JavaScript, in hidden worlds too; the logo, Home and Blog links stay in the page's language. Code-like strings (the draft's file name, shell commands, punched tape text) stay in English in both locales; the words they stand for are on the page.
- **Accessible names in animated type:** a display name split into per-letter spans for its entrance (Avant-garde) carries the whole name once in an `sr-only` span and hides the letters from assistive tech; word-split names (Editorial) keep real spaces between the words; Cartoon's hand-lettered titles, name, and credits do the same as Avant-garde. Decorative devices that repeat the page (Engineer's seams and pager, Terminal's prompt text in headings, every tape strip and mark, Cartoon's caricature, iris, and ampersand, Clay's set, stills, coils, tag brackets, ruler, window title bars, commits, key glyphs, and FPS meter) are hidden from assistive tech.

### Named Rules
**The Keycap Rule (Clay).** Every control in Clay is a clay keycap: a face in its clay over a side wall of the same clay in shade, and a press that sinks the face into the wall by `translate` alone, on twos, never by changing colour alone or by reflowing. An action that sends is an Enter key with its return glyph; a toggle prints its plus or minus.

**The Dev Objects Rule (Clay).** Everything on the sweep is an object from a developer's desk, pressed in clay: keycaps, editor windows, a git branch, a keyboard, sticky notes, folders, tags. Film-set grammar (slates, takes, clapperboards, wraps, frame counters) is retired at the user's request; the stop-motion lives only in how things move.

**The On Twos Rule (Clay).** Clay moves like stop-motion: every animation is `steps()` or a 12 fps clock, poses held, never tweened; the set renders only on that clock and only while seen.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** declare a new world with the nine color tokens, two font slots, and two radii on its `[data-design]` selector (overriding `--on-accent` or `--accent-text` only if its accent misses 4.5:1, and adding world-only tokens only for its own material), register it in `src/data/designs.ts`, add its `design.<id>` name in both locales, and add it to the anti-FOUC allowlist and both visibility selector lists in `global.css`.
- **Do** keep the accent on buttons, labels, rules, and states (The One Accent Rule), and set text on an accent fill with `text-on-accent`, never a hardcoded white or page color.
- **Do** treat Signal Orange as a display color: rules, outlines, fills with Ink text, and text at 24px and up through `.text-accent-display`. Small orange text goes through `--accent-text` (Signal Orange Ink, #bf3900); raw Signal Orange measures 3.0:1 on Newsprint and 3.3:1 under white text.
- **Do** keep Tape Orange off text inside a White window (2.79:1); set window copy in Tape Black ink and let orange underline or rule there (The Window Ink Rule).
- **Do** resolve headings through the heading slot and body through the body slot in shared components; a world's own classes live in `src/styles/worlds/<world>.css` and may name its faces there.
- **Do** render both locales in every text node behind `data-lang-show` so the language toggle never re-renders.
- **Do** respect `prefers-reduced-motion`, and make new motion join the reveal grammar (fade, 30px rise, 0.5s, once) or the world's own signature.
- **Do** fork a shared section only when a world's identity needs it, inside the shared section with `data-design-hide` / `data-design-show` (The Same Bones Rule).
- **Do** carry state as a mark or a word as well as a colour (Tape's band and cross, Terminal's "●" and "Present", `aria-pressed`, `aria-expanded`).
- **Do** give a new Cartoon card a field, a text colour, a safe colour, and a display colour from the measured pairs (The Field Rule), and keep Paper on Vermilion to display type (3.4:1).
- **Do** animate Cartoon on held poses (`steps()`), never tweened, and keep its depth to overlap (The Paper on Paper Rule).
- **Do** mount a WebGL scene only while its world is active, calibrate a lighter phone build, and ship a fallback, as Avant-garde (a CSS fallback) and Clay (the copy alone on the sweep) do.
- **Do** set Clay's chrome on Sweep Raised (#14b2a7) in Sweep Shadow (5.44:1) or Clay Ink (6.67:1), with Sweep Line borders, as the design switcher does.
- **Do** set every word on clay in Clay Ink and keep Pink a fill, an underline, a bead, or a coil (The Ink on Clay Rule); build a new clay surface from the full Lit Clay recipe and press it by hand (unequal radii, a small tilt).
- **Do** make a new Clay control a clay keycap with its side wall that sinks on twos (The Keycap Rule), give a new Clay surface its own patch of the tile (`--tx`, `--ty`, `--ts`), and give a new Clay animation `steps()` or the 12 fps clock (The On Twos Rule).

### Don't:
- **Don't** mix tokens across worlds. No candy surface in Engineer, no Creative shadow in Editorial, no prompt green outside Terminal, no Tape Orange outside Tape, no Cartoon field outside Cartoon (The Own Palette Rule).
- **Don't** fork content per world. Copy, data, order, and links come from `src/data` and `src/i18n` only; a world may restage them, never rewrite or reorder them.
- **Don't** add a fill to Engineer beyond the Email key and reverse video, or a size beyond the text size and the hero title (The Reverse Video Rule, The One Size Rule).
- **Don't** punch decorative noise into Tape: every strip encodes its text (The Holes Are Data Rule).
- **Don't** give a Cartoon card a shadow, a gradient, a blur, a frame, halftone dots, or comic-book lettering effects: it is cut paper, not a comic page or a neo-brutalist card. Strokes stay drawn lines on tickets and tags in the card's own colour.
- **Don't** label a Cartoon title from above; a credit is the name over its function in small caps (The Credits Rule).
- **Don't** set Pink text anywhere in Clay (1.02:1 on the sweep), cast a black or hard-offset shadow, lay a flat untextured clay, or tween a Clay motion; and don't bring back film-set grammar (slates, takes, wraps) or drift into the isometric low-poly dev room: it is a developer's desk, handmade, on one painted sweep (The Dev Objects Rule).
